## Problem

A decisão 0010 (spec 1138, `docs/decisions/0010-custo-de-delegar-o-commit-ao-git-commit.md`) mediu que cada `Skill("quenching:git:commit")` do passo 5e de `/quenching:specs:execute` carrega ~11,3k chars (~2,8k tokens) — 6,4k deles repetindo leituras que o passo 5d já fez —, contra ~0,1k de um commit determinístico com o mesmo contrato: ~62k tokens numa spec de 22 tasks contra ~2k. A recomendação (parar de delegar por task e commitar por um `cq git commit` determinístico) ficou registrada como follow-up, e `cq git` não tem verbo `commit` hoje.

Consequência: todo execute, e todo spec-runner sob condutor, segue pagando a recarga por task.

## Proposal

- `cq git commit --subject "<subject>"` exists: it commits the existing index with that subject and reports the new sha and the subject git actually recorded.
- The verb never stages, never amends, never passes `--no-verify`, and refuses (exit 2) on an empty index or a blank subject.
- Step 5e of `/quenching:specs:execute` commits each task through `cq git commit`, with no `Skill("quenching:git:commit")` call per task; the subject is the one the conventions already loaded in step 5d name.
- `/quenching:git:commit` stays the entry point for a human commit, unchanged.
- The execute command, its `execution.md` reference and its test agree on the new chain, and the Codex translation is regenerated from them.

## Out of Scope

- Changing `/quenching:git:commit` or `/quenching:git:commit-incremental`: they stay the human-facing path, per decision 0010.
- Making the verb resolve the subject from the spec: step 5d already holds the conventions, and putting the grammar in a second place would duplicate it.
- Staging files inside the verb: the contract is "the commit path never stages on the caller's behalf".
- Other callers of `Skill("quenching:git:commit")` (`pr/review.md`, `knowledge/documentation/produce.md`): they commit once, not per task, so the measured cost does not apply.

## Design

- **Decision: a verb, not "load once".** Decision 0010 recommends one deterministic `cq git commit` call and names "load the body once" as the fallback: the verb costs ~0.1k tokens per task against ~2.8k, and keeps the commit boundary in a tool rather than a re-read prose body. The rule: a per-task commit boundary is a tool call, not a delegated command body.
- **Decision: the caller supplies the subject.** The verb takes `--subject` and never derives one; the grammar and any declared `gitConventions.commitSubject` are applied by execute from what step 5d already loaded. The verb reports `git log -1 --format=%s` after committing, so execute still compares the recorded subject (commit.md §Where the subject can fail).
- **Decision: the verb is exactly the commit contract.** Commits the existing index only; hooks enabled; no `--amend`, no `--no-verify`; empty index or blank subject is exit 2; a failed commit (hook) is exit 1 with git's message, HEAD untouched.
- **Binding contracts:** `assets/references/git/commit.md` §Commit messages §The subject is the anchor (subject recorded by `cq specs task --check --subject`, sha by `--commit`); the `git` pillar emits through `emit` and exits 0/1/2 per `common/output.py`.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Keep the delegation:** rejected, ~62k tokens against ~2k on a 22-task spec (decision 0010).
- **Load the git:commit body once per execution:** rejected as fallback only; it saves nearly as much but keeps a 4.9k body in context for the whole run.
- **A bare `git commit -m` in the chain:** rejected; the boundary would live in prose with no refusal on an empty index and no reported subject to compare.

## Open Decisions

- Whether the verb should also tick the spec task (`--task`). Decided by whether a later measurement shows the two-call chain costs anything; left out now because the tick belongs to `cq specs`.

## Risks

- A hook that rewrites the subject breaks the anchor: unchanged from today; the verb reports the recorded subject so execute can report a mismatch as a finding.
- A caller passing a subject that departs from the conventions: ACCEPTED — the verb is deterministic by design and the conventions step stays in execute's 5d, where it already was.
- A pre-existing session that quoted the old chain in its context keeps calling the Skill: harmless, `git:commit` still works.
