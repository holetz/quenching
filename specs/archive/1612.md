## Problem

Encontrado pelo mapa da rodada 17 do autopilot (2026-10-12) e confirmado pelo condutor na leitura, em `main`
a963e946. O `_registry_lifecycle` (`plugins/quenching/assets/bin/quenching/ops/inventory.py:116-126`) atribui um
lifecycle a qualquer linha do registry em que apareça a palavra solta "active" ou "archived", pegando o primeiro
token em crase terminado em `.py`/`.sh`/…. Só que o próprio escritor (`ops/registry.py:40-47`, `_entry_row`) grava na
mesma linha a coluna Purpose, que vem do docstring do script.

Entrada: `scripts/prune.py`, sem `_archive` no caminho, com o docstring "Delete active sessions". O
`cq ops registry --write` grava ``| `prune.py` | Delete active sessions | `/prune` | — | … |``. Na leitura seguinte,
`prune.py` sai com lifecycle `active`, embora a coluna de lifecycle diga "—". Quando as duas palavras aparecem,
"active" vence, e um script de `_archive/` é lido como ativo.

Consequência: o `cq ops registry --check` acusa `stale` (exit 1) num registry que acabou de ser escrito, e o
`--write` seguinte grava um lifecycle que ninguém declarou (o `lifecycle` entra no digest, `model.py:54`). Com isso,
o `/quenching:ops:align` nunca converge numa passada, e as checagens que dependem do lifecycle rodam sobre um estado
inventado. A correção lê o lifecycle só da coluna de lifecycle das linhas de tabela, ou de uma declaração explícita
fora da zona gerada, nunca da linha inteira.

## Proposal

- `_registry_lifecycle` reads a lifecycle only from the Lifecycle column of a table row, or from an explicit `` `path` — active|archived `` declaration line, never from the whole line.
- A registry written by `--write` never makes `--check` report stale because a Purpose contains "active" or "archived".

## Out of Scope

- Changing the registry format or the digest: the writer is correct and stays as it is.
- Other readers of the registry: only `_registry_lifecycle` is wrong.

## Impact

- `plugins/quenching/tests/test_ops.py` — the regression test

## Design

- Rule: lifecycle is data of a position, not a word on a line. A table row yields the cell under the header named `Lifecycle`; a non-table line counts only when it matches `` `path` — active|archived `` as a whole. Prose inside a Purpose cell can therefore never declare one.
- Alternative weighed: stripping the Purpose cell before matching; rejected, it still reads any other cell and leaves the whole-line shape.
- Assumption: the existing declaration form used by the tests (`` `run.py` — active ``) is the "explicit declaration" the design means.

## Alternatives Considered

- Have the writer sanitize the Purpose: rejected, the Purpose is authored content and the reader is what trusts the wrong thing.

## Open Decisions

- none — the design fixes the reading rule and the declaration form.

## Risks

- A hand-written registry that declared lifecycle in prose on a table-less line in another shape stops being read. ACCEPTED — the explicit form is the one the tests and the writer document.
