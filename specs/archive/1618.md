## Problem

Reproduzido pela revisão de correção do épico 1616 (2026-10-12), em `main` b6c1d7f5, com `cq ops doctor --json` em
alvos descartáveis, comparando com a963e946. O leitor de lifecycle do registry de ops
(`plugins/quenching/assets/bin/quenching/ops/inventory.py`), endurecido pelas #1614 e #1617, ainda perde formas
documentadas ou comuns:

1. **(regressão desde a #1614)** Uma célula de estado em code span, no estilo da tabela do próprio
   `references/ops-align/lifecycle.md:18`: `` | `run.py` | `active` | ``. A a963e946 dava `run.py -> active`; a
   db5a7136 e a b6c1d7f5 dão `op-orphan: entry point `run.py` has no declared lifecycle`. Também se perdem
   `archived 2026-08-30` (data sem separador), `archived since …` e `**archived**`. As causas são o `_STATE_REASON`
   (`:108`), que exige separador ou fim de texto logo após o estado, e o `:144`, que procura o caminho só em `cells[0]`.
2. **(já existia)** A declaração fora de tabela não aceita maiúscula, ao contrário da coluna:
   `` `old.py` — Archived (2026-08-30) `` e `` - `run.py` — Active `` dão `op-orphan` (`:148-149`, `re.fullmatch` sem
   `re.I`).

Consequência: entry points com o lifecycle declarado viram o erro "no declared lifecycle", e scripts arquivados
deixam de ser reconhecidos. Isso faz o `/quenching:ops:align` pedir declarações que já existem. A correção normaliza
a célula ou a declaração antes de ler o estado (tira crases e ênfase, ignora maiúsculas) e aceita o estado como a
primeira palavra seguida de qualquer texto, mantendo que o texto livre do Purpose nunca é lido como lifecycle.

## Proposal

- The registry lifecycle reader normalizes a Lifecycle cell (code span, `**`/`_` emphasis, case) before reading the state, and takes the state as the first word with any text after it.
- The entry-point path is taken from the first cell of the row holding a code span that ends in a script extension, not only from the first cell.
- A declaration line outside a table accepts any case and styling of the state, and a date or `since` directly after it.
- Purpose free text is never read as a lifecycle: only the Lifecycle column and the explicit `` `path` — state `` declaration form are read.
- One table of cases in `test_ops.py` covers the forms of 1612, 1615 and 1618.

## Out of Scope

- A fourth round on the reading contract: if the next review finds a regression in this reader, the conductor sends the accepted declaration formats to the human epic (epic 1619 Design).
- Changing `references/ops-align/lifecycle.md` or the registry writer: the reader aligns to the documented forms, not the other way around.

## Design

Conductor decision (epic 1619): fix the root cause by normalizing before reading. `_plain` strips backticks and emphasis and lowercases. A Lifecycle cell matches `(active|archived)\b.*`. A declaration line matches the state followed by end, a separator, a digit or `since`, so prose such as "active sessions" outside a table is still not a lifecycle. Cell text beyond the first word is free in the Lifecycle column because that column is the explicit declaration.

## Alternatives Considered

- One more regex case per form: rejected, it is the third round of that pattern (1612, 1615, 1618).

## Open Decisions

- none — the epic 1619 Design settled the approach; declaration lines keep a restricted tail so prose is not read as a lifecycle.

## Risks

- A Lifecycle cell such as "active sessions" now reads as active. ACCEPTED — the column is the explicit lifecycle declaration, and the previous test asserting the opposite was changed on purpose.
