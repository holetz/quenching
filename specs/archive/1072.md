## Problem

The conductor does not yet integrate the `toolchain` and `delivery` fronts in the declared order or publish the corrected seven-front operating model and surface claims. The integration must make applicability and profiles explicit, redraw rather than append the operating diagram, name the security pillar while skipping it, and rerun the ordering proof.

## Proposal

- Integrate `toolchain` and `delivery` into the conductor in the declared seven-front order.
- Make applicability and target profiles explicit in the conductor stage, preserving the probe-before-inventory rule.
- Redraw the operating-model diagram and update README, command catalog, explanation and bundle surface claims to name seven fronts and the security pillar.
- Rerun the ordering proof against the declared source and keep `git` and `security` outside the converging-front sequence.

## Out of Scope

- Implementing either new front or the security pillar is out of scope; C2, C4 and C5 own those routes.
- Changing the underlying front contracts or configuration loader is out of scope; this spec integrates their completed surfaces.
- Absorbing the plugin's own release path into `delivery` is out of scope.
- Adding product-code architecture as an eighth front is out of scope and remains closed by A1.

## Design

The conductor runs local fronts in one declared dependency order and treats applicability as an explicit stage before inventory. `security` and `git` are named in the surface model as pillars but are not passed through the front conductor. The operating model is redrawn from the source contract rather than appended to stale prose, and copied descriptions are updated at their source and intentional mirrors.

The integration consumes the completed routes without reimplementing their probes. The ordering test asserts the declared sequence and pillar exclusion, while the README and explanation checks assert published claims against a source that can fail when a front is added or removed.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Append the two fronts to the existing conductor list.** Rejected because the order is dependency-driven and the operating model needs a redraw.
- **Add `security` as the eighth conductor row.** Rejected because a pillar converges nothing and must remain outside the front pipeline.
- **Patch only the ordering test.** Rejected because tests cannot repair stale user-facing claims or applicability documentation.
- **Let each command decide applicability independently.** Rejected because the conductor must establish profile applicability before it inventories fronts to avoid duplicate and contradictory decisions.

## Open Decisions

- The exact placement of profile applicability in the conductor's user-visible report is decided by the completed front command contracts, while the stage itself remains before inventory.
- Whether the README should retain any historical count is decided per sentence under A2+A3's computed-fact rule; current claims must derive from the declared surface.

## Risks

- **The conductor invokes a new front before its route is complete.** Mitigation: C6's prerequisites are the approved C2 and C4 specs, and the ordering test runs after route registration.
- **Published claims are updated inconsistently.** Mitigation: update source and copied surfaces together and run citation, mirror and surface tests.
- **A pillar is accidentally made convergable.** Mitigation: assert that `security` is named but absent from the conductor sequence and ships no front-only artifacts.
- **The operating diagram preserves stale rows.** Mitigation: redraw it from the seven-front declaration rather than appending two nodes.
